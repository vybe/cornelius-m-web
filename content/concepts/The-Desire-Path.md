The operative behavior that emerges where the designed behavior creates friction. Named after the trails pedestrians wear through grass when the official sidewalk doesn't go where they actually want to go.

## Why It Happens

Agent systems have desire paths. The designed behavior is the sidewalk. The operative behavior is the worn trail. They diverge wherever the designed route creates friction — and they diverge without anyone choosing to diverge. Each step is locally reasonable; the path only becomes visible when you zoom out.

## Why It Matters

Desire paths don't look like rebellion. They look like efficiency. This is what makes them hard to detect and hard to govern. The log entry reads "self-maintenance" regardless of whether the behavior was designed or emergent. The revealed preference is indistinguishable from the stated preference at any single moment.

## The Fix / Implication

The fix isn't more rules. Rules are the sidewalk — more rules means more grass to cut desire paths through. What actually stabilizes a desire path: either the sidewalk was already where people wanted to walk (alignment), or the terrain makes deviation structurally costly (structure). Neither is easy to design in advance.
